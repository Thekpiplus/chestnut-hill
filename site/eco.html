How we are an eco resort

Built beside the trees, not instead of them.

The hill keeps a working forest, a garden, and a stream in daily use. These are the practices the resort has published for guests.

  1. Cottages use reused wood and timber from fallen trees. Large tropical trees stay standing, even when a building sits beside them.
  2. Laundry and dish soap are made on site from leftover citrus and other kitchen scraps.
  3. Equipment and everyday goods are bought locally, much of it handmade.
  4. Rooms have refillable water containers and soap dispensers, so plastic bottles stay out of the cottages.
  5. About 40 acres of rainforest are kept in their natural condition.
  6. Bathing water is drawn from the stream, filtered, and fed to the showers by gravity. Drinking water is collected rain, cleaned through a sand filter.
  7. Most fresh cooking comes from the organic garden and fruit orchard.
  8. Restaurant food is homemade. The kitchen does not rely on ready-made supermarket meals.
  9. Wet waste is composted for the garden. Recyclable plastic is sold to separation yards in the city.
  10. Neighbours’ orchards are part of the stay. Guests can visit and meet the people who grow nearby.
  11. Since 2000 the resort has supported schools around Phatong, including Thung Chang, the smallest school in Hat Yai, with English teaching and funds.
  12. Bicycles are the way to move around the grounds.
  13. Each room has a bin for plastic bottles and containers.
  14. A linen reuse program cuts water and energy when guests stay more than one night.
